The buyer pathway

Understand the property.

Buying as is generally means accepting the property in its present condition, subject to the written agreement and applicable legal requirements.

Before making an offer:

  • Review available property and pricing information.
  • Consider the property’s location and condition.
  • Investigate repair and rehabilitation needs.
  • Confirm occupancy, title, taxes, and potential liens.
  • Discuss inspections and closing arrangements.
  • Consult qualified legal, title, and real estate professionals.

1. Agreement to purchase

Buyer offers to purchase the property identified above for the stated purchase price, subject to Seller’s written acceptance and any additional written terms agreed upon by both parties.

2. As-is condition

Buyer understands that the property is offered in its present, as-is condition. Unless otherwise agreed in writing, Seller is not obligated to repair, renovate, improve, or replace any part of the property before closing.

3. Inspection and due diligence

Buyer may inspect the property and investigate its condition, occupancy, title, taxes, restrictions, and other relevant matters during any period agreed to in writing. Any right to withdraw based on inspection findings must be expressly stated in a signed agreement.

4. Disclosures

Seller remains responsible for disclosures required under applicable law. Acceptance of the property as is does not authorize fraud, intentional concealment, or other conduct prohibited by law.

5. Closing and payment

Closing shall occur on a mutually agreed date through an authorized settlement provider. Deposits, payment method, financing conditions, closing costs, possession, and related requirements shall be established in a separate written agreement or according to applicable local practice.

6. Written modifications

Any change to these terms must be written and signed by both parties. This sample is provided for demonstration purposes and should be reviewed by a qualified attorney before use.
